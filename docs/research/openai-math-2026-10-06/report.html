Which of the new OpenAI mathematics results can help PETLS?

There are worthwhile connections, but no reviewed result establishes a drop-in faster persistent-Laplacian solver or a topology-preserving spectral simplification. The best feature lead is degree-controlled graph null ensembles; the clearest new experimental method is nonlinear smoothing of topology descriptors. The most immediate improvement uncovered during this review is independent of the new papers: separate finite-field homology from real spectral rank. A reproducible simplicial torsion example exposes a mode-selection failure in the current working tree.

The library’s actual mathematical contract

For a filtered inclusion Ka ⊆ Kb, let Bq(a) be the boundary on the q-simplices present at a. Partition Bq+1(b)Bq+1(b)T into old and newly added q-simplex coordinates. The implementation uses the generalized Schur complement:

U(b) = [ A   Cᵀ ]       L_q(a,b) = B_q(a)ᵀ B_q(a) + A − Cᵀ D† C
       [ C    D ]

At a=b this is the ordinary combinatorial Hodge Laplacian. For genuine chain complexes over the reals, its nullity is the rank of Hq(Ka;ℝ) → Hq(Kb;ℝ). Positive eigenvalues add structural information; a kernel basis supplies real harmonic representatives. These statements require the same coefficient field and compatible boundary maps.

The formula is implemented in core/laplacian.py:129. The current partial solver already applies a sparse operator with exact Schur correction, uses float64 Torch operations on the requested device, and maintains X=LY to stay in range(L), with a small working block independent of harmonic multiplicity: core/low_modes.py:46 and core/low_modes.py:157. The eliminated D block is still densified and inverted/pseudoinverted at lines 77–86. Persistent harmonic localization still requires the full dense operator, guarded for size: core/complex.py:1404.

Ranked opportunities

Ranking reflects fit and implementation value for this repository, not the mathematical importance of the papers.

1 · Degree-controlled topology and spectrum null ensembles — family 131, with 113 follow-up

Polynomial mixing of the switch chain for every graphical degree sequence

New claim: Theorem 1.1 gives polynomial mixing for the specified lazy switch chain on every labelled simple-undirected degree sequence: total-variation distance 1/4 by at most 2n⁸ proposals. The exact transition kernel matters. The separate exact-uniform sampler has expected polynomial bit cost.

Library use: ask whether a graph’s holes, positive spectrum or harmonic participation reveal organization beyond its degrees. Sample null graphs, build a chosen incidence/clique construction consistently, and compare predeclared statistics through the existing analysis APIs. This is a focused interpretation feature, not new eigensolver mathematics. Existing edge swapping predates this release; the claimed novelty is the all-degree-sequence guarantee.

Limits: the theorem does not cover directed/weighted graphs, connected-only rejection, or chemically valid molecules. Short chains are not certified uniform, correlated samples are not independent, and 2n⁸ is impractical as a default burn-in. Rewiring each scale independently does not produce a nested filtration. Start with an example reporting proposals, acceptance, seed and sampling limitations. Family 113’s host-constrained prescribed-degree sampling corollary is a follow-up for forbidden edges, with its own approximation/runtime assumptions.

Stronger constrained variant: A Fully Polynomial Randomized Approximation Scheme for Perfect Matchings in General Graphs Corollary 10.1 (pp. 41–44) supplies approximate counting and sampling of subgraphs with prescribed vertex degrees inside an explicitly listed simple undirected host. In feasible cases the sampled law is within total-variation distance τ of uniform, with every-execution bit cost polynomial in input length and τ⁻¹. This is a closer model for spatially allowed edges: the host can forbid implausible contacts. Connectivity, weights, direction and chemical validity still require separate treatment. The Tutte-gadget/count-guided sampler is research implementation work, not a fast existing library routine; it does not prove mixing of a host-restricted switch chain.

Verification: Declared Lean scope for 131 covers the chain estimates, explicitly excluding the exact sampler. Declared Lean scope for 113 lists the perfect-matching counting FPRAS for this route, not the host-constrained sampling corollary. That corollary’s reduction and sampling argument were inspected in the PDF. Neither sampler was independently implemented or validated.

2 · Nonlinear smoothing of trajectory descriptors — family 332, experimental

Metric Markov Cotype Two of ℓ1

New claim: Theorem 1.2 proves metric Markov cotype two for ℓ₁, with squared constant 3024. For fixed-length vectors xᵢ and a reversible Markov matrix A with stationary measure π, it bounds the cost of moving xᵢ to smoother yᵢ plus their one-step variation by the original multistep variation. Sections 5.1–5.3 supply an explicit construction:

p = 1/(t+1),  q = t/(t+1),  G = p(I − qA)⁻¹
y_i = E[coordinatewise median(x_J1, x_J2, x_J3)],  J1,J2,J3 iid from G_i.

Library use: an optional analysis example for aligned, fixed-grid spectral or basis-invariant localization descriptors across molecular frames. Compare the median construction against ordinary averaging and unsmoothed descriptors, measuring whether real transitions survive noise suppression. Finite descriptors lie in ℓ₁, so this method has a concrete implementation path.

Limits: the output is a processed descriptor, not exact topology. It need not be realizable by a complex, and it preserves neither persistence intervals nor integer Betti counts. Raw harmonic vectors have sign/rotation ambiguity and must not be smoothed this way. Monte Carlo evaluation introduces error absent from the exact-expectation theorem. No Lean coverage was located at the pinned snapshot. Treat this as an experiment, not a new default.

3 · High-dimensional sparse topology fixtures — family 177

Bounded-degree coboundary expanders in every dimension

New claim: Theorem 1.1 constructs arbitrarily large bounded-vertex-degree d-dimensional complexes, d≥3, with uniform 𝔽₂ coboundary expansion below d, using normalized weighted support norms. Reduced 𝔽₂ cohomology below d vanishes.

Library use: nontrivial sparse higher-dimensional fixtures to stress boundary assembly, numerical nullity and positive modes as size increases. For finite complexes, this vanishing also forces reduced real homology to vanish in those degrees. The top degree and intermediate filtration stages are not covered.

Limits: the theorem does not provide a numerical gap for the real unweighted Hodge Laplacian. 𝔽₂ acyclicity does not exclude odd torsion visible over the library’s 𝔽₁₁ field. The algebraic coset construction can be enormous and has no demonstrated practical instance here. First assess small-instance feasibility; do not commit to a production generator just from existence. Declared Lean scope for 177 matches the higher-dimensional existence result, not Torch numerics.

4 · Spectral calibration graphs — families 178 and 219

Deterministic nonbipartite Ramanujan graphs in every fixed degree
GOE bulk universality for fixed-degree random regular graphs

178: the claimed construction yields simple nonbipartite d-regular Ramanujan graphs for fixed d≥3 and sufficiently large even n. Since L₀=dI−A, every positive ordinary graph eigenvalue lies in (d−2√(d−1), d+2√(d−1)). A pure graph complex also has β₁=m−n+1 and the same nonzero spectrum for B₁ᵀB₁ and B₁B₁ᵀ. That combination supplies large-kernel solver fixtures. Do not clique-complete the graph and expect the same H₁. The construction’s polynomial exponent depends on d; practical performance is unestablished.

219: fixed-degree random regular adjacency spectra have the claimed GOE bulk limit after density unfolding. This transfers algebraically to ordinary L₀. Use it for ensemble-level bulk diagnostics, not the smallest positive eigenvalue, spectral edges, arbitrary molecular graphs or persistent q-Laplacians. It is asymptotic, with no usable finite-n acceptance threshold established here. Neither family has declared Lean coverage located in this snapshot. Existing graph fixtures already provide much of the benchmarking benefit.

5 · Controlled graph-metric preprocessing — family 089

Planar Graph Metrics Embed into L1 with Constant Distortion
L1 Embeddings of Graphs of Bounded Treewidth

New claim: constant-distortion L₁ embeddings for shortest-path metrics of finite connected planar graphs, and a treewidth-dependent bound for bounded-treewidth graphs, with positive edge lengths.

Library use: a graph-metric Rips example and, if a practical embedding can be constructed, experiments quantifying filtration distortion. For an actual rescaled map satisfying d≤d′≤Dd, the elementary inclusions are Ripsd(r/D) ⊆ Ripsd′(r) ⊆ Ripsd(r). They control scale multiplicatively. This deduction is ours; it is not a persistent-Laplacian result from the paper.

Limits: exact graph-distance Rips is already supported. The paper is not a ready compact GPU embedding algorithm. Barcode-scale control gives no automatic error bound for positive spectra or harmonic vectors. The L₁ metric must enter through distances=. Family 094 cannot be chained after this result: it excludes p=1. Declared Lean scope for 089 describes existence/distortion, not a performant implementation.

Focused research leads, with missing bridges

365 · Boundary-response signatures. Determination of a metric and a unitary connection from one boundary patch concerns smooth metrics and U(2) connections on compact manifolds of dimension ≥3, recovered from all measurements on a boundary patch. It suggests studying selected-site response matrices S=LBB−LBILII†LIB across scale. The algebra is classical Kron/generalized Schur reduction and closely matches current code. A response descriptor could complement global spectra for selected molecules or interfaces. The continuum uniqueness theorem does not establish discrete identifiability, numerical stability, or uniqueness from eigenvalues. Declared Lean scope for 365 covers a different, rough-conductivity nonuniqueness companion.

337 · Filling-volume descriptors. Sharp integral fillings in CAT(0) spaces treats compactly supported integral n-cycles, n≥2, in proper CAT(0) spaces and gives the sharp Euclidean filling-mass upper bound. A possible research module would distinguish a cavity’s homology class from a geometric measure of its ambient filling. But real harmonic vectors are not automatically integral currents; a filling in ambient space need not lie in the filtered complex; and a periodic torus is not globally CAT(0). For ordinary embedded closed surfaces in ℝ³, the relevant Euclidean isoperimetric information is already classical. No practical filling algorithm or new crystal-analysis guarantee follows. Declared Lean scope for 337 covers the integral-current result.

374 · Transport-coordinate stability. Sharp One-Third Stability of Brenier Maps gives sharp 1/3-Hölder stability of quadratic optimal maps from a uniform continuous convex-body source to compactly supported target measures; atomic targets are allowed. It can inform a future fixed-reference point-cloud representation, but is not an empirical-to-empirical matching theorem or barcode-Wasserstein stability theorem. Its L² control does not supply the pointwise/Hausdorff control needed to transfer directly to Rips persistence. New optimal-transport infrastructure and a new stability bridge would be required. Declared Lean scope for 374 covers the asserted exponent and sharpness.

Immediate mathematical improvements, independent of the release

First: stop using 𝔽₁₁ Betti numbers to index the real spectrum

Gudhi homology uses 𝔽₁₁ in core/complex.py:1071. The low-mode path sets rank=rows−betti and the complete branch slices eigenvalues starting at betti in core/low_modes.py:235 and core/low_modes.py:275. These are different fields; torsion can make those counts disagree.

The supplied reproduction constructs a degree-11 mapping cone with one stellar subdivision: 38 vertices, 138 edges and 101 triangles. Boundary composition is exactly zero. Independent checks give β(𝔽₂)=(1,0,0), β(𝔽₁₁)=(1,1,1), and real L₁ nullity zero. Its first two real eigenvalues are 0.01725544720792108 and 0.017539702983241444.

8 positive modesRange iteration finds the first positive value, approximately 0.01725544724.
24 positive modesComplete branch skips the first value and begins at approximately 0.01753970298. Existing diagnostics detect the field mismatch, mark modes unavailable and return no gap.

This is a confirmed omission and avoidable unavailable result, not a silently accepted wrong certified gap. Keep modular homology as an independent output, expose its field, and determine real spectral zeros/rank separately. Multiple modular primes can be diagnostics, but are not universally exact rational-rank certificates. Preserve small genuine positive modes when designing the rank/tolerance policy.

Next: basis-invariant localization

core/complex.py:1444 returns arbitrary numerical kernel columns. If H is an orthonormal harmonic basis, H and HQ for orthogonal Q are equally valid. The projector P=HHᵀ and its diagonal sᵢ=ΣⱼHᵢⱼ² do not depend on that rotation. Expose total harmonic participation on simplices/molecules, with completeness flags when only part of the kernel is available. A projector does not identify individual barcode intervals; cross-frame comparison still needs a common coordinate mapping, and subspace stability requires separation from positive modes.

Then: sparse Schur solves and explicit spectral approximation contracts

The remaining dense eliminated block is a concrete scaling target. First investigate factor solves instead of explicit inverses where appropriate, caching for repeated pairs, and compatible iterative solves for singular D. Any approximate correction must keep symmetry/PSD, quantify error on the original operator and control small positive modes. Theorem 174 does not supply that contract.

A useful research target is (1−ε)L ⪯ L̃ ⪯ (1+ε)L on the same chain coordinates. Such a PSD quadratic-form bound with ε<1 preserves the kernel and controls eigenvalues. It still needs a coherent filtration-level construction; an isolated fixed-scale bound is insufficient for a persistent pipeline. None of the screened candidates establishes this for the library’s general persistent higher-dimensional operators.

Additional foundations

Validate Bq−1Bq=0 for imported boundaries and δ²=0 for sheaf restrictions before describing kernels as homology. General block-valued sheaves, weighted Hodge inner products and periodic/temporal complexes are coherent future features, but no reviewed paper removes the need to define their topology, adjoints, filtrations and numerical contracts carefully.

Recommended order of work

  1. Correctness: decouple modular homology from real mode selection; retain the torsion reproduction as a regression case if a fix is implemented.
  2. Interpretability: add complete-kernel participation/projector summaries, and distinguish basis visualization from feature identity.
  3. First paper-driven feature: a degree-controlled graph-null analysis example, with explicit sampling assumptions and no claim of chemical realism.
  4. Small research experiment: implement the 332 median-resolvent smoother outside exact topology, testing noise reduction against lost transitions.
  5. Performance research: investigate compatible sparse Schur solves using known numerical methods; use graph fixtures first, and assess feasibility before adopting the 177 algebraic construction.
